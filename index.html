The Game

You are the engineer AND the pilot.

Every build decision matters: where you place your core, how you protect your movement parts, where you mount your weapons. Damage happens physically, block by block — with chain reactions, parts breaking off, and a spectacular collapse when the core goes down. Your bot, your fault, your triumph.

Features

Destruction is the spectacle.

No hidden health bars, no fake damage. What you build is exactly what gets shot at.

🧱

True Block Damage

Every block has its own health. Hits chain into neighbouring blocks, cut-off parts break away and fall, and losing your core means watching your whole bot come apart.

🔧

Deep Building

Grid-based construction with mirror mode, real connection logic and advanced armor techniques. Where a block is attached decides how damage travels through your bot.

🕷️

Wheels, Hover & Spider Legs

Three drive systems with completely different feels: grippy wheels with drift handbrake, terrain-hugging hover pads, and spider legs that climb where wheels give up. And more movement systems are on the way.

⚡

Energy Warfare

Your core is your power plant. Unload everything in one devastating plasma salvo — or play the long game with sustained laser fire. Alpha strike vs. endurance is a build choice.

🤖

Enemies That Fight Back

AI opponents keep their distance, circle you, dodge and retreat when damaged — built from the same bots you can build yourself. They're not geniuses yet, but they get smarter with every update.

🛡️

Fair by Design

Combat power is never for sale. Bigger, stronger bots are earned by playing, never bought — and matches are balanced by bot size, so victory comes down to how you build and how you pilot.

Story

A war for a broken Earth.

Earth is lost — abandoned by humanity, claimed by machines. Two factions now fight for what remains. Unlike the classics that inspired it, Fight for GAIA gives every match a reason: a place on the ruined Earth, a faction, and a campaign to fight for.

Aurora

The reclaimers. As one of Aurora's drone operators, you build and pilot combat bots in the fight to take Earth back — mission by mission, ruin by ruin.

Umbra

The machine army that stands in your way. Relentless, adaptive, and holding every strategic point on the planet. Their bots come apart just like yours — if you shoot well enough.

The Promise

Combat power is never for sale. Progression gives you options, not advantages. A story campaign and co-op you can enjoy solo or with friends — no dead lobbies, no matchmaking hamster wheel.

Development

Devlog

Fight for GAIA is an indie game in active development, built with Godot 4. Here's what's new — full devlogs are posted on Discord.

Devlog #11

Godot Port · August 1–5
  • Big step: engine switch. GAIA now runs on the Godot Engine — a full rebuild, with the old version kept as reference. A restart with everything we've learned.
  • Almost everything is already back. Five days in: the full building system, auto-save, the hangar menu — plus a proper inventory panel and a system menu, new on top.
  • Driving — better than before. Rebuilt suspension without jitter, slopes, drift & donuts, crab walk, jumps, recovery. Already feels better than the old build.
  • Shooting is back. Laser online, energy from the core, hits tear bots apart block by block — chain reactions included.

Devlog #10

Build v2.0 · July 23–29
  • Spider legs — a new way to move. One block = one leg: the more you attach, the more spider you get. Legs climb where wheels give up; tap Space to jump, hold to fold and slide.
  • Honest driving. Wheels spin out on steep slopes, walls are walls, and a real drift handbrake lets you slide sideways at speed.
  • Recovery reworked. The self-righting move now has a cooldown and works whenever your drive loses the ground.
  • First external playtest. Two testers, honest feedback — plasma salvos already cost far less energy.
